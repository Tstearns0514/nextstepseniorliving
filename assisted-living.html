Senior Living Options

Assisted Living

A residential setting that combines housing, daily-life support, meals, activities, and access to staff while helping residents maintain as much independence as possible.

Talk Through Your Situation →

Who may want to explore this option?

Assisted living may be worth exploring when someone needs regular help with daily activities or benefits from having staff available around the clock, but does not require a hospital or nursing-home level of medical care.

See the NextStep Process →
What It Can Include

Services and support vary by community.

These are common examples. Individual communities differ in staffing, services, policies, pricing, and what needs they can support.

1

Personal Care

Assistance may include bathing, dressing, grooming, toileting, mobility, and other activities of daily living.

2

Medication Support

Communities may offer medication reminders or medication-management services depending on state rules and resident needs.

3

Meals & Daily Routine

Dining, housekeeping, activities, transportation, and wellness support are commonly part of community life.

When to Explore It

Signs it may be worth learning more.

  • Bathing, dressing, grooming, or mobility are becoming harder.
  • Medication routines are difficult to manage consistently.
  • Family is providing more daily support than can be sustained.
  • Living alone is creating safety, nutrition, isolation, or routine concerns.
